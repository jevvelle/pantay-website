Project GEIS (Gender Equality Index for Schools)

2023–2025

A school’s commitment to inclusion becomes sustainable when it is reflected not only in individual activities, but in its policies, facilities, learning environment, and systems for protecting students.

Through GEIS, PANTAY supported Sucat Senior High School in strengthening its student handbook, learning resources, reporting and referral mechanisms, gender-responsive facilities, and student participation, while using the Rainbow Report Card to assess and improve its SOGIESC responsiveness. These changes demonstrate how targeted investment can help a school build systems that continue to support students beyond a single project or training.

Investing in inclusive education means investing in a generation of students who can learn, participate, and thrive without discrimination.

PANTAY recognizes Sucat Senior High School’s commitment and gratefully acknowledges Embassy of Netherlands in the Philippines, Commission on Human Rights, and National Anti-Poverty Commission Youth and Students Sector for supporting this work.

Project ACCESS: Active Student-Champions of a Campaign for Empowerment on SRHR in Schools

2025–2026

Young people need more than information about their rights—they need the skills, confidence, networks, and resources to act on them.

Through Project ACCESS, PANTAY supported students and youth leaders at Universidad de Manila Bahaghari to strengthen their knowledge on SOGIESC, SRHR, advocacy, and community engagement, and to translate these into youth-led campaigns and organizing. Their participation has since extended into local youth governance spaces, including the Manila Youth Development Council, demonstrating how investment in youth capacity can create pathways into civic leadership.

When we invest in young people, we are not only supporting today’s advocates; we are building the next generation of people who can shape decisions in their communities.

This work was made possible through the partnership of PANTAY, Universidad de Manila and Bahaghari, with support from DKT Philippines Foundation.

Strengthening queer leadership and representation in local governance

Queer leaders do not only take a seat at the table—we bring our communities, experiences, and priorities with us.

Read the full story

Equality Initiatives Program (EQUIP) supported by National Democratic Institute

Pinoy LGBTIQ+ POWER Batch 1: Progressive Officials Working for Equality and Rights supported by Outright International

The journey of POWER Fellow Councilor Miel Agustin of Malolos City, Bulacan reflects the importance of investing in young queer leaders who can bring LGBTIQ+ representation and priorities into political leadership.

Through PANTAY’s leadership development and support under Projects EQUIP and POWER, Miel strengthened her capacity to engage in governance and advocate for issues affecting her community. Today, as a local queer elected official in Malolos, Bulacan, she brings queer representation and SOGIESC concerns into local governance, including efforts around SOGIE awareness activities and the city’s Anti-Discrimination Ordinance. As part of her POWER capstone, Miel also developed an economic empowerment initiative that provided livelihood support to queer community members, showing how queer leadership can respond to the everyday needs of the communities it represents. Miel’s story shows why continued investment in young queer leaders matters: when we believe in their leadership and equip them to participate, they can help redefine political spaces, bring queer priorities into governance, and create opportunities for others to lead. PANTAY recognizes Councilor Miel Agustin and the partners who have supported her leadership journey, with gratitude to National Democratic Institute and Outright International for investing in young queer leaders and their participation in governance.

The Rainbow AGENDA: Advancing Gender Equality Narratives to Drive LGBTIQ+ Awareness for the 2025 Philippine Midterm Elections

2024–2025

LGBTIQ+ people participate in elections as voters, candidates, campaign workers, and community members, yet discrimination, stigma, and harassment can limit their meaningful participation.

Through Rainbow AGENDA, PANTAY worked with communities to document these concerns, develop LGBTIQ+ rights agendas, engage candidates, and bring these priorities into dialogue with electoral institutions. COMELEC’s openness to partnership and continued engagement with civil society provided an important space for these community concerns to be heard and translated into institutional action, contributing to the Anti-Discrimination and Fair Campaigning Guidelines for the 12 May 2025 elections, which included protections for people of diverse SOGIESC. The experience shows that policy change is strengthened when communities bring their lived experiences and evidence, while institutions remain open to dialogue and are willing to translate these into stronger protections.

PANTAY recognizes COMELEC’s commitment to a more inclusive electoral process, as well as the communities, partners, and Embassy of Canada in the Philippines whose support made sustained advocacy and engagement possible.

From Seedlings to Forests: Rethinking Support for Youth-Led Organisations in Asia

By Reden Raz, Executive Director at PANTAY, Marat Yu, Regional Programme Director, Asia at EMpower, and Tamanna Basu from Better Funding

Movements by youth, for youth, and of youth are the answer to a world grappling with a polycrisis. ‘Youth issues’ are often limited to education and employment, but the truth is that youth issues are intersectoral, intersectional, and global. This is exactly why young people belong in leadership positions, boardrooms and decision-making spaces.

Philanthropy must recognise this. If the sector wants to respond to the complex challenges of the twenty-first century, it must follow the leadership of young people. Traditional models have to give way to something more flexible, trust-based, participatory, and values-driven. A core rethinking is required around questions of who holds power, who defines priorities, and what sustained support looks like.
